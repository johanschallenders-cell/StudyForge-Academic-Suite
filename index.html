Understanding US Academic Grading Systems

Grade Point Average (GPA) is a standardized metric used in American high schools and universities to summarize student academic achievement. It is calculated by converting letter grades (A, B, C, D, F) into a numerical scale, typically ranging from 0.0 to 4.0.

Difference Between Weighted and Unweighted GPA

  • Unweighted GPA: Measured on a standard 0.0 to 4.0 scale regardless of course difficulty. An "A" in an introductory class and an "A" in an Advanced Placement (AP) course both yield 4.0 points.
  • Weighted GPA: Accounts for academic rigor. Honors courses often add 0.5 points (up to 4.5), while AP and IB (International Baccalaureate) courses add 1.0 point (up to 5.0).

How Final Exam Predictors Work

Your overall class grade is calculated using a weighted average formula. The final exam grade calculation formula is:

Required Score = (Target Grade - (Current Grade × (1 - Exam Weight))) / Exam Weight
